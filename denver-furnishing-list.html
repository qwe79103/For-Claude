Before you buy anything

This can swing the budget by $1,500

Ask the landlord exactly what stays in the house. US rentals almost always include a fridge, oven/range and dishwasher — but microwave, washer and dryer and window coverings are all coin flips. A washer/dryer pair alone is $700–1,200.

  • Washer and dryerIncluded, or just hookups? This is the single biggest unknown.
  • MicrowaveOften not provided. Budgeted below at $90–150.
  • Blinds or curtains on every windowUsually blinds are there. If not, add $100–200.
  • Refrigerator, range, dishwasherConfirm anyway, in writing.

On skipping the bed frame

Fine in Denver — the dry air makes the mould risk far lower than in Taiwan. But don't put the mattress directly on the floor: body moisture still can't escape downward, and floors are noticeably colder in a Denver winter. A bunkie board or slat base at $50–90 lifts it a few inches and solves both. It's the cheapest item on this page with the biggest payoff.

Buy all four of these used. Tables and chairs are bulky, boring, and barely depreciate once you've bought them second-hand — which means you'll sell them for close to what you paid when you leave.

One exception worth paying for: a real task chair. Eight hours a day on a dining chair becomes a back problem. Denver has an unusually good used market for Herman Miller and Steelcase from office closures — $150–300 for a chair that cost $1,200 new.

What it actually costs you

You're here two years, so the number that matters isn't what you spend — it's what you don't get back. Expect to recover roughly 25–30% on things bought new, and 50–60% on things you bought used in the first place.

So the honest range is $70–145 a month on top of rent. Against $3,000+ rent, the difference between lean and comfortable is small — which argues for buying decent things and enjoying them.

Parked, not forgotten

Two things sit outside these totals. Lighting is handled — the house has enough, so that's $120–255 you don't spend. Humidifiers are excluded for now at your call, but keep them in mind: Taipei runs 70–80% humidity and Denver is often 20–30%, lower indoors once the heating is on. If the first few weeks bring nosebleeds, cracked lips or broken sleep, a bedroom unit is about $60 and fixes it. Easier to buy once you know whether you need it.

The split that works

  • New: anything touching your body — mattress, pillows, bedding, towels. Also anything cheap new, like kitchen basics.
  • Used: tables, chairs, desks, dressers, lamps, bookshelves. Bulky, durable, and they hold their resale value because you already bought at the bottom.
  • Sell it all before you fly home. List in April or August when students are arriving. Start four weeks out, not four days.

Where to go in Denver

  • IKEA Centennial — about 25 minutes south. Best value for new tables, chairs, lamps and storage.
  • Costco — mattresses, towels, cookware, kitchen basics. One trip covers a lot.
  • Facebook Marketplace and OfferUp — where the real savings are. Denver has a big transient professional and student population, so quality used furniture moves constantly.
  • ARC Thrift and Habitat for Humanity ReStore — cheap, hit or miss, worth one visit.
  • Target — good for the fiddly household items in one go.
A small piece of luck

US tariffs on furniture currently hit upholstered seating and kitchen cabinets at 25%, with increases scheduled for January 2027. Plain wooden furniture — tables, non-upholstered chairs, desks — isn't covered. By skipping the sofa you've skipped the one category that's been getting more expensive.

Prices are US dollars, for the Denver metro, as of October 2026. Treat them as planning figures — mattress pricing in particular swings hard on sales, so check before you buy. Budget a little slack: nobody has ever furnished a house for exactly the estimate.
